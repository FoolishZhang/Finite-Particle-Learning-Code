# Defensible current statement

Independent nuisance observations leave the exact statistical task unchanged but can alter the learning preference of a finite-prior-particle objective. Correcting its bank expectation does not remove shared-bank randomness. In the known two-coordinate Gaussian-logistic correlation model with a=1, R<1, and K>=2, the variance of a fixed-parameter path-gradient average decomposes exactly as sigma_Y²/N + sigma_Z²/B + sigma_e²/(NB), with sigma_Z² strictly positive for finite n and K. Thus, for fixed B, even exact mean calibration leaves positive expected joint KL for any measurable global-minimizer selection of the infinite-data, finite-bank objective. The statement concerns an interior teacher and a fixed C1 penalty independent of training banks; it provides no practical lower bound or SGD guarantee. The classical variance decomposition is not claimed as a new statistical principle.

With a=1, n=254, K=256 and the same 16 previously generated complete datasets of N=4096 observations, fresh nested banks reduced full-calibration parameter MSE to the same-data numerical MLE from .00505094 at B=128 to .00122021 at B=512. The paired difference has a simultaneous 95% development-approximation interval [.00102831,.00663314]. Separately, 8192 independent calibration banks evaluated at only six model points yielded one quadratic coefficient, c=.00837193. At B=512, its teacher-averaged joint-KL difference from full calibration is 3.09282e-6, with upper bound 6.71100e-6 below the prespecified 1e-4 noninferiority margin. Both new primary gates pass; the previous study's two failed gates remain unchanged.

This is a computational intervention on reused data, not a fresh-data replication. Intervals condition on frozen calibrations, and teacher-averaged noninferiority does not establish teacherwise or uniform guarantees. In this panel, marginalization has smaller discrepancy to the same-data MLE. Given the existing r table, MLE computation is simpler; no end-to-end timing comparison was performed. The study supports a mechanism paper separating objective bias, calibration, and particle precision, rather than a general superiority claim for a new calibration algorithm.
