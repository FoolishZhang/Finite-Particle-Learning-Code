# Independent nuisance observations can destroy simulated-likelihood consistency

In a correctly specified Gaussian–logistic family with a fixed positive logit variance floor, appending independent observations with a known, parameter-free distribution can make empirical minimization of the raw prior-particle likelihood learn spurious correlation. The failure persists when the inner particle count grows linearly and the number of outer banks grows polynomially with dimension.

The teacher consists of independent fair bits. Candidates learn only the correlation rho of a known two-coordinate active block; the n nuisance coordinates have the same independent distribution under every candidate. Fix a>0 and a finite correlation grid containing 0 and ±R, with 0<R<1. Set K=n+2 and B=ceil(n^(1+eta)) for fixed eta>0, sharing complete prior banks across all observations and candidates. Conditional on any prescribed complete data set, the raw empirical grid minimizer selects ±R with probability tending to one. The conditional failure bound is uniform over data sets; it is not a simultaneous guarantee for one bank realization over every possible data set.

The learned integrated-model KL consequently tends to a positive constant. When the label vectors are iid draws from the null teacher and independent of the banks, exactly marginalizing nuisance latent factors inside the likelihood restores consistency using the same observations, candidate models, bank coupling and N/B/K: the grid estimator selects the truth and its expected KL tends to zero. The prescribed label count N_n need only diverge, arbitrarily slowly. These statements retain the known active block, fixed grid, iid teacher data independent of the banks, growing K, and exact empirical minimization assumptions.

There is also a finite-budget learning consequence. At a=1, n=254, N=1024 and B=8192, raw training selects the correlation endpoints in 14/16 and 15/16 independent runs for K=2 and K=4. Analytic model-risk bounds, exact binomial arithmetic and exhaustive label-count enumeration yield simultaneous 95% lower bounds of .003656 and .004290 nats on the raw program's expected excess joint KL over a fixed table-likelihood baseline, rounded outward. These bounds do not require a rigorous Gaussian quadrature certificate. A separate paired K4 test has 13/16 analytically guaranteed improvement events and p=.01064; it tests an event probability, not the size of a mean improvement.

Estimation noise is distinct from objective bias. At a fixed nonzero leading-gradient point with K=2, the population gradient is of order n^(-1/2). Price-gradient variance is of that same order, whereas standard Cholesky pathwise variance without a control variate tends to a positive constant. Relative consistency respectively requires and is implied by B/sqrt(n)→infinity and B/n→infinity. Price estimates have a non-Gaussian Poisson-integral limit at the critical budget. A nuisance-softmax control variate also attains the smaller sufficient budget, so Price is not uniquely capable of variance reduction. All these estimators target the same gradient; more accurate estimation does not remove its objective preference.

Marginalization is not a complete fixed-K repair. K2 shows almost no risk reduction at the tested budget. A declared post-hoc enumeration suggests that the active conditional expected-bank target itself remains sensitive to finite labels. This is a qualified numerical-table proxy, not a rigorous infinite-bank optimizer certificate, and it does not revise the original tests.

The reported risk evaluates the integrated observed model Q; the raw objective evaluates the expected log risk of a random K-particle predictor. The result concerns total joint KL and fixed active events, not nonvanishing per-coordinate average loss. It does not cover unrestricted full-dimensional covariance learning, unknown active locations, continuous optimization, SGD, or natural-data performance.
