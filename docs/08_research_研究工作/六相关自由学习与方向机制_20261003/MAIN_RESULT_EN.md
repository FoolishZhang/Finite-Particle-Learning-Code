# Six free correlations: representation-sensitive risk persists

We release all six physical correlations in a known four-variable block and initialize every learner at the identity. Sixteen new data streams, N=4096, K=100, and 512 updates give mean integrated joint KL of .0063134213 for fixed nuisance scales, .0014017301 for learned scales, and .0009340534 for analytic factorization. Both alternatives improve on FIXED in all sixteen streams. The original simultaneous development intervals support the two improvements, but all three training precision targets fail; FREE-to-FACT noninferiority is not established. The combined requirements are 0/3, and no earlier failure is retroactively passed.

The population extension covers the full spectrally compact correlation class. With nuisance scales fixed at one and at least two but at most polynomially many prior particles, the rescaled centered objective converges uniformly to a strictly concave potential. Its minimizers are extreme points. Applying known correlation-matrix rank geometry shows that at least two eigenvalues reach the .1 floor and, for this truth, population o(w)-near-minimizers have integrated-risk liminf at least 9/12800. This is not a finite-n Adam theorem, and the covariance C remains full rank.

A separate fresh Monte Carlo study finds all eighteen prespecified directional effects, but none meets the absolute precision target. Shrinking the uniform nuisance scale from one to .2 increases full-weight overlap from about .309 to .902 while reducing the outward gradient at the true covariance. After alignment by each true correlation sign, the reference-mean-mark contribution becomes more negative, but an opposing weight–mark coupling term cancels more of it. This is a measured use of an exact Price decomposition, not a new universal sign diagnostic; the .2 profile is not the learned FREE scale vector.

Known block location, fixed means/variances, and zero active–nuisance cross covariance remain. No neural network is trained and v6 is unchanged. See REPORT (historical reference not distributed), [RESULTS](RESULTS.md), and THEORY (historical reference not distributed).
