# Defensible core statement

In the known two-coordinate Gaussian-logistic correlation model with a=1 and fixed 0<R<1, independent nuisance observations can distort finite-prior-particle learning while leaving the exact statistical task unchanged. For growing n, 2 <= K_n <= C0 n^p, and correctly specified iid complete observations independent of both prior pools, a quantitative transfer through the finite-calibration slope b yields sufficient training and independent Price-calibration budgets Bw²→infinity and Cw→infinity, where w=J_K/sqrt(nv). Together with N→infinity and controlled implementation/optimization errors, these conditions give vanishing full joint KL. A refining finite-grid construction is supplied under exact known r values and real arithmetic. These are not necessary rates, SGD guarantees, or certification of the present fixed 101/801 grids.

For K=n+2, one can use B=n and C=ceil(sqrt(n)). With the same training banks, the calibrated estimator converges to the teacher, whereas raw learning converges to the same-sign endpoint with inflated magnitude for a nonzero interior teacher, or to the endpoint set for a null teacher, retaining positive risk. Fewer additional calibration banks do not imply equal total computation or superiority over known-structure MLE.

Fresh complete observations from 16 base streams, N=4096, cover n in {62,254} crossed with K in {64,256}. All four prespecified teacher-averaged local-versus-full calibration noninferiority gates pass; the largest simultaneous upper bound is 1.48227e-5 below the 1e-4 margin. Full-calibration parameter MSE to the same-data numerical MLE decreases from .00863918 at B=128 to .00219368 at B=512, passing the fifth gate. These are development-approximation intervals conditional on frozen calibration pools, not teacherwise or uniform guarantees. At fixed K=256, B=512 and a null teacher, increasing n from 62 to 254 changes raw endpoint selection from 0/16 to 16/16, while marginalization and MLE remain unchanged for each realization.

The evidence supports a mechanism paper separating objective bias from computational precision, not a general superiority claim for a complex calibration algorithm. Earlier failed gates remain unchanged. The sealed v4.1 manuscript and PDF are preserved.
